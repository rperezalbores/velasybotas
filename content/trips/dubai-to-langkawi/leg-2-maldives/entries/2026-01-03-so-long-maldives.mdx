January 3rd. Crossroads Marina. The forecast to Galle was good, the family had a ferry to catch, and we had 400 miles of ocean ahead of us.

The departure split cleanly in two. The family — María Eugenia, Eugenia, Sofía — took the ferry to Malé, then the gate to Dubai. Twelve days aboard: a marlin, manta rays, a New Year on the beach. They left with salt in their hair. María Eugenia filmed from the ferry as Artemisa slipped out of the marina and turned south. One boat going home. One heading on.

In Galle, we will welcome Paul onboard. A good sailor and a good man, joining as third crew for the leg everyone who knows these waters calls the difficult one — Sri Lanka across the Bay of Bengal, through the Andaman, into Thailand. More weather, more distance, more to get wrong. We'd need the extra pair of hands.

---

But first, a word about the Maldives.

The beauty is real and it is extraordinary. The water colour alone — that particular turquoise that shifts to deep cobalt at the edge of the atoll — is worth crossing an ocean for. The coral, the fish, the silence of an anchorage at sunset with nothing around you but lagoon and sky. We were lucky to be here. We knew it every day.

And then there is the other side of the Maldives — the one the brochures skip.

**The anchorages.** The atolls here are a geological peculiarity: the ocean floor drops to forty metres in the middle, and the islands rise from it almost vertically, like columns. The result is that you are either anchored in forty metres of water — which no anchor chain handles gracefully — or you're in two metres, which is not enough for Artemisa. The narrow band of usable depth in between exists, but finding it requires patience, good charts, and a willingness to drag anchor, try and perhaps try again. Cruising the Maldives in a keelboat is beautiful. It is also, frequently, a puzzle.

**The bureaucracy.** The Maldives requires an agent. This is not optional — it is how the system works, and Assad from Yacht Link Maldives was excellent. But the agent is there to navigate a paperwork architecture that could only have been designed by a committee with too much time and a fondness for fees. A non-exhaustive list of what you pay to sail in paradise: an anchorage fee, a sanitation fee, a government fee, a sailing permit fee, an entry fee, an exit fee, a transponder fee, and the agent fee that holds all the others together. Each one arrives on a separate form. Each form requires a stamp. Some require two.

We paid them all. We smiled. The Maldives, when it hands you a manta ray at two metres, makes you forget every one of them.

But when the window to Sri Lanka opened, we didn't need to think twice.

Pero primero, unas palabras sobre las Maldivas.

La belleza es real y es extraordinaria. El color del agua solo — ese turquesa particular que vira a cobalto profundo en el borde del atolón — ya vale cruzar un océano. El coral, los peces, el silencio de un fondeo al atardecer con solo laguna y cielo a tu alrededor. Tuvimos suerte de estar aquí. Lo supimos cada día.

Y luego está la otra cara de las Maldivas — la que los folletos omiten.

**Los fondeos.** Los atolones aquí son una peculiaridad geológica: el fondo del océano cae en picado a cuarenta metros en el interior, y las islas emergen de él casi en vertical, como columnas. El resultado es que o fondeas en cuarenta metros de agua — lo que ninguna cadena de ancla gestiona con gracia — o estás en dos metros, que no es suficiente para Artemisa. La estrecha franja de profundidad útil existe, pero encontrarla requiere paciencia, buenas cartas y disposición a garrar el ancla a las 3 de la madrugada y volver a intentarlo. Navegar las Maldivas en un velero de quilla es precioso. También es, con frecuencia, un rompecabezas.

**La burocracia.** Las Maldivas exigen un agente. No es opcional — así funciona el sistema, y Assad de Yacht Link Maldives fue excelente. Pero el agente existe para navegar una arquitectura de papeleo que solo pudo diseñar un comité con demasiado tiempo libre y afición a las tasas. Una lista no exhaustiva de lo que pagas por navegar en el paraíso: tasa de fondeo, tasa de saneamiento, tasa gubernamental, permiso de navegación, tasa de entrada, tasa de salida, tasa de transpondedor, y la tasa del agente que lo une todo. Cada una llega en un formulario distinto. Cada formulario requiere un sello. Algunos requieren dos.

Las pagamos todas. Sonreímos. Las Maldivas, cuando te pone una mantarraya a dos metros, te hace olvidar cada una de ellas.

Pero cuando se abrió la ventana hacia Sri Lanka, no tuvimos que pensarlo dos veces.
